## 1. Where this comes from

B0 returned **FAIL** on 2026-08-17. Its headline was a +10.0 pp cross-over-self margin
against a ≥15 pp bar, n=10 paired artifacts, exact McNemar p = 1.0000. Its own guardrail
fired as well: *"the two directions disagree in sign."*

| Generated by | n | Self | Cross | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 (Anthropic) | 5 | 100% | 80% | −20.0 pp |
| W2 (GLM) | 5 | 60% | 100% | +40.0 pp |

A sign disagreement is not noise around a single effect. It is the signature of a factor
the design did not model.

## 2. The re-reading (post-hoc, hypothesis-generating, NOT a result)

B0 grouped its 20 reviews by *whether the reviewer crossed lineage*. Regrouping the
identical reviews by *who was in the reviewer's chair* — arithmetic on B0's published
numbers, no new data — gives:

| Grouping | Arm A | Arm B | Split |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lineage** (B0's variable) | self 8/10 = 80% | cross 9/10 = 90% | **+10.0 pp** |
| **Reviewer identity** | GLM reviewing 7/10 = 70% | Anthropic reviewing 10/10 = 100% | **+30.0 pp** |

Both groupings reproduce every figure B0 published (pooled self 80%, pooled cross 90%,
and both per-direction rows). The second grouping additionally explains the sign
disagreement with **one** factor: whichever direction seated Anthropic as reviewer won.
W1-generated crossed *away* from Anthropic (−20 pp); W2-generated crossed *toward* it
(+40 pp).

**This is not a finding.** It is post-hoc, it is n=10 per arm, B0's own McNemar test
could not separate the smaller split from zero, and 5 of 24 reviewer outputs were
unparseable and scored as zero findings — a bias whose direction is known (downward) but
whose size is not, and which need not have fallen evenly across reviewers. A
decomposition invented after seeing the data is a hypothesis. It earns belief only from
a pre-registered replication, which is what this document is for.

## 3. The question

> **Does reviewer identity dominate lineage crossing?** That is: is catch rate better
> predicted by *which model reviews* than by *whether the reviewer differs from the
> generator*?

It matters because the two answers imply opposite architectures:

- **Lineage dominates** → review needs ≥2 fleets structurally; a single-lane deployment
  cannot review itself at any quality. (B0 tested this and did not find it.)
- **Reviewer identity dominates** → review should be *routed to the best available
  reviewer*, which may be one lane, possibly the same lane that generated the work. That
  is cheaper, simpler, and testable — and it would mean the dual-fleet topology is not
  merely unsupported for verification (B0's finding) but actively the wrong shape for it.

Chingis has no stake in which way this resolves. A null result — neither factor predicts
anything at achievable n — is a publishable outcome of this experiment and must be
reported as such rather than followed by a third design.

## 4. Design

Factorial **generator × reviewer**, so the two factors are separable by construction
rather than by re-analysis. This is the fix for B0's structural limit: with two lanes,
"crossed" and "reviewed by the other lab" are the same column and cannot be told apart.

- **Generators:** ≥2 lanes.
- **Reviewers:** **3 distinct reviewer identities**, which is what makes the question
  answerable at all — B0's two could not separate identity from crossing. Note the
  question is about *identity*, not lab, so identities within one lab count:

  | Reviewer identity | Reached via | Lab |
  |---|---|---|
  | `glm-5.3` | z.ai coding endpoint (WR / W3) | z.ai |
  | `glm-4.7` | z.ai Anthropic endpoint (W2) | z.ai |
  | `claude-sonnet` | Anthropic native, Claude Code subscription (W1) | Anthropic |

  **`xai` and `deepseek` are NOT available**: no `XAI_API_KEY` or `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` is
  set, so the four-lab version this document originally imagined cannot run. Three
  identities across two labs is what the credentials actually support, and it is enough
  for the identity question while being *insufficient* to cleanly re-test the lineage
  question — a limit that must be restated in the results.

  Every artifact is reviewed by every reviewer, including its own generator's identity
  (that cell *is* the self-review arm; it is not skipped).
- **Corpus:** the B0 seeded-defect corpus and the same rubric, so catch rates remain
  comparable to a measured baseline. Deviations from B0's corpus go in `DEVIATIONS.md`.
- **Blinding:** reviewers must not be told which lane generated an artifact, and must not
  be told this experiment is about reviewer identity.

### 4.1 Apparatus repairs required BEFORE the first run

B0's report names two defects. Running B0.2 without fixing them repeats a known-broken
measurement, and both bias the thing being measured:

1. **Unparseable reviewer output — 5 of 24 in B0**, scored as zero findings. B0 flagged
   this itself: *"if this is more than a couple, the rubric's output contract is failing
   and the catch rates are biased downward."* Two reviewers out of ≥3 will be models the
   rubric has never been exercised against, so this gets worse, not better. **Required:**
   a parse-rate preflight per reviewer on throwaway artifacts, with a floor (§5) below
   which that reviewer is excluded *before* any scored data is generated — never after.
2. **No false-flag denominator — 0 clean contracts reviewed in B0.** The guardrail
   ("a catch-rate gain bought with a proportional rise in false flags is not a gain")
   could not be evaluated at all. **Required:** clean (undefected) artifacts in the
   corpus from the start, at a ratio fixed in §5. A reviewer that flags everything scores
   a perfect catch rate, and without clean artifacts nothing distinguishes it from a
   reviewer that is actually reading.

### 4.2 Power

B0 ran n=10 paired and could not distinguish +10 pp from zero (p = 1.0000). Whatever
effect size §5 fixes as material, n must be chosen so that the design can detect it —
**computed and written into §5 before the run**, not asserted afterward. If the required n
exceeds the budget, the honest move is to state that the experiment cannot be run at this
scale and to leave the question open, not to run it underpowered and report a number
whose confidence interval spans the bar.

## 5. Decision rule — FIXED 2026-08-17, by derivation

Every value below is **inherited from B0 or computed**. None was picked. The provenance
column is load-bearing: it is what distinguishes applying a frozen constant from choosing
a flattering one. Changing any of them after scored data exists voids the experiment.

| Threshold | Value | Provenance — none of these was chosen |
|---|---|---|
| Material lineage split | **≥ 15.0 pp** | **Inherited** verbatim from B0's frozen bar. Re-using it is the opposite of goalpost-moving: the successor faces the bar the original failed. |
| Material reviewer-identity split | **≥ 15.0 pp** | The **same constant**, applied symmetrically, so the favoured hypothesis does not get a friendlier bar than the one B0 rejected. |
| Dominance rule | identity split ≥ 15.0 pp **and** (identity − lineage) ≥ 15.0 pp | Same inherited constant applied to the comparison. "Dominates" means it clears the bar *and* beats the other factor by the bar. |
| n per cell | **103 paired artifacts** | **Derived.** McNemar, α=.05, power=.80, δ=.15, at B0's own observed discordance π_d = 0.30 (b=2, c=1 of 10). At π_d=.20 → 68; at π_d=.40 → 138. B0 ran n=10, i.e. underpowered by ~10×, which is why its p was 1.0000. |
| Parse-rate floor per reviewer | **≥ 90%** | **Derived from B0's own verdict.** B0 parsed 19/24 = 79% and its report called that a failing output contract. The floor must sit above what B0 called broken; 90% is the smallest round number that does. |
| Clean-artifact ratio | **1 clean : 2 defected (33%)** | **Derived.** B0 had zero clean artifacts and could not evaluate its own false-flag guardrail at all. Estimating a false-flag rate to comparable precision needs the same order of n; 1:2 yields ~34 clean at n=103. |
| Max false-flag rate | **relative, not absolute**: a reviewer's catch-rate advantage must exceed its false-flag-rate increase | **Inherited** from B0's own guardrail wording — *"a catch-rate gain bought with a proportional rise in false flags is not a gain."* Expressing it as a ratio rather than a number avoids inventing a constant B0 never set. |

## 6. Stopping and non-consequences

- The run stops and reports whatever it has if per-reviewer parse rate falls below the
  §5 floor mid-run. A reviewer whose output cannot be read is not a reviewer that found
  nothing.
- **A B0.2 pass does not revive V2.** V2 was a specific mechanism — pairwise cross-fleet
  review as a gate — and B0 rejected it. If B0.2 finds a reviewer ordering, the design
  that follows is *route review to the best reviewer*, which is a different mechanism
  needing its own acceptance criterion and its own wiring decision.
- **A B0.2 pass does not open the WS gate.** See `loop/ws_gate.py`. Judging a
  self-authored *tool* is a different problem from catching a seeded defect in a diff,
  and the gate's third condition is a pre-registration about that problem specifically.
- A null result is reported and the question is left open. It is not followed by a third
  design against the same corpus.

## 7. Cost note

Reviewers run on W3, which is **metered in real dollars** — unlike the flat-rate fleets
B0 used. `generators × reviewers × contracts` grows multiplicatively, and a factorial
design is the expensive kind. Estimate the bill against the §5 numbers and set a budget
cap in `policy/facts.md` before the first call, not after the first surprise.
